Why Everyone Is Still Moving to South Carolina in 2026, And What That Means for Homeowners

South Carolina is not just growing. It is being chosen.

June 29, 2026 · 5 min read · The Tyler Simmons Team

Originally published in The Simmons Newsletter on June 29, 2026. Figures reflect data available at that time.

From Columbia to Charleston, Myrtle Beach to Greenville, Lexington to Florence, more people are looking at the Palmetto State and seeing something they want more of: space, opportunity, community, and a lifestyle that feels easier to enjoy.

And the numbers back it up.

According to state analysis of U.S. Census Bureau data, South Carolina grew by 1.5 percent between July 2024 and July 2025, making it the fastest growing state in the country by percentage growth. The state added nearly 80,000 people during that period, and the biggest driver was domestic migration, with 66,622 more people moving into South Carolina from other states than leaving it.

That is a big deal for real estate.

Whether you already own a home, are thinking about selling, are considering buying, or know someone relocating to South Carolina, this growth is shaping the market in real time.

Why are people still moving to South Carolina?

There is no single reason. That is part of what makes South Carolina so attractive.

For some people, it is the weather. For others, it is the cost of living compared with larger metro areas. Some are moving to be closer to family. Some are relocating for jobs. Others are choosing South Carolina because they want access to the beach, lakes, small towns, major employers, college communities, and a slower pace without giving up convenience.

United Van Lines’ 2025 National Movers Study ranked South Carolina as one of the top inbound states, with 61 percent of tracked moves into the state. The study also found that Americans moving across state lines were most often motivated by being closer to family, a company transfer or new job, and retirement. It also pointed to a broader shift toward smaller cities and towns where housing may feel more attainable than in larger markets.

That description fits South Carolina well.

We have communities where you can be close to downtown dining, hospitals, universities, major employers, military bases, beaches, lakes, and quiet neighborhoods, often within a short drive of each other.

Jobs and opportunity are part of the story

Relocation is not just about lifestyle. People also move where they see opportunity.

South Carolina announced $9.12 billion in capital investment in 2025, representing the third highest year for industry recruitment on record, along with more than 8,100 new jobs. Rural recruitment also made up more than 40 percent of announced capital investment and new jobs, which shows growth is not only happening in the largest cities.

That matters because job growth and housing demand are closely connected.

When new employers come in, workers follow. When workers follow, they need homes. When families move, they look for schools, neighborhoods, commute routes, restaurants, parks, churches, and community. Over time, that creates ripple effects for homeowners, buyers, sellers, investors, and local businesses.

South Carolina still offers a mix of options

One reason South Carolina continues to attract attention is that it offers different types of living within the same state.

You can find a more affordable city market in Columbia, a coastal lifestyle in Myrtle Beach, a luxury and historic feel in Charleston, a growing downtown and business scene in Greenville, and strong suburban demand in places like Lexington, Irmo, Blythewood, Sumter, Florence, and surrounding communities.

Realtor.com’s April 2026 statewide data showed a South Carolina median listing price of $355,000, with 57,428 homes for sale, a median rent of $1,825 per month, and a median of 54 days on market. The same data showed Columbia with a median listing price of $260,000, Myrtle Beach at $279,000, Greenville at $385,000, and Charleston at $650,000.

That variety is important.

Not every buyer wants the same thing. Not every seller is competing with the same market. A relocating family comparing Columbia and Charleston is looking at two very different price points, lifestyles, and inventory conditions. A seller in Lexington may have a different strategy than a seller near the coast. A first time buyer in Columbia may see opportunity that looks very different from a buyer shopping in a higher priced coastal market.

That is why local guidance matters.

What does this mean for South Carolina homeowners?

For homeowners, population growth can be a positive long term sign.

More people moving in means more potential demand for housing, especially in areas with strong schools, convenient commutes, new jobs, medical access, shopping, dining, and lifestyle amenities. But growth does not automatically mean every home will sell quickly or for any price.

The 2026 market is more strategic than the fast moving frenzy many people remember from a few years ago.

Redfin’s May 2026 South Carolina housing data showed home prices up 1.7 percent year over year, home sales up 5 percent, and homes for sale up 8.2 percent. In other words, buyers are still active, but they also have more choices than they did in tighter inventory markets.

That means homeowners should pay close attention to three things.

First, pricing matters. Buyers are still buying, but they are comparing more options. Overpricing can cause a home to sit, while smart pricing can create stronger activity.

Second, presentation matters. Clean photos, strong marketing, curb appeal, staging, and clear property details can help a home stand out.

Third, local strategy matters. Statewide trends are helpful, but your neighborhood, price range, condition, location, and competition determine your real selling position.

What does this mean for buyers?

For buyers, South Carolina’s growth is a reminder that waiting for the “perfect” market can be tricky.

Mortgage rates are still a major part of affordability. Freddie Mac reported the average 30 year fixed mortgage rate at 6.48 percent as of June 4, 2026, down from the previous week and lower than the same time one year earlier.

Even with higher rates than buyers saw a few years ago, the market is not frozen. People are still relocating. Homes are still selling. Sellers are still making moves. And buyers who are prepared can still find good opportunities.

The key is knowing where to look, what to ask, and how to structure a strong offer without overextending.

For some buyers, that may mean looking at Columbia, Florence, Sumter, or surrounding areas for affordability. For others, it may mean paying more for coastal access, historic charm, or a specific school zone. The right answer depends on your goals, timeline, budget, and lifestyle.

What does this mean for sellers?

For sellers, the growth story is encouraging, but it should not create false confidence.

Yes, people are moving here. Yes, South Carolina remains desirable. Yes, many homeowners have gained equity over the past several years.

But buyers are more informed now. They are watching mortgage payments, taxes, insurance, condition, repairs, location, and resale potential. They are not just buying any home at any price.

That is why a strong listing strategy is so important. A good strategy should answer questions like:

  1. What are buyers currently paying for homes like mine?
  2. How many similar homes are competing with mine?
  3. What repairs or updates would make the biggest difference?
  4. Should I list now, later this summer, or wait?
  5. What will I realistically net after closing costs, payoff, and preparation?

The best sellers in this market are not guessing. They are preparing.
